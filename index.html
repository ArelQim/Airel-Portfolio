How this site was built responsibly

The practical decisions behind every page here — because a portfolio about IT should model good IT practice.

privacy & consent

Nobody appears without permission

Classmates and collaborators shown in screenshots or named in case studies gave written consent beforehand. Any demo data is synthetic or anonymized.

copyright

Every asset is licensed or original

Images, icons, and fonts used here are either self-made, royalty-free, or explicitly licensed for reuse, with sources listed in each project's credits.

responsible ai use

AI assistance is disclosed, not hidden

Where I used an AI tool for drafting, debugging, or ideation, it's noted in the relevant case study, along with what I changed or verified myself.

account & site security

Every screenshot and repo link was reviewed to remove API keys, passwords, and internal URLs before publishing. 2FA is enabled on all accounts tied to this site.

/reflection
The hardest part of this project wasn't building the portfolio — it was deciding what didn't belong in it.

Early drafts of this site included full-resolution screenshots straight from our capstone system, before I noticed a teammate's name was visible in a database record I hadn't meant to show. That small mistake changed how I approached everything after: I started treating "is this safe to publish" as its own step in the workflow, not something to check at the end.

I also had to get comfortable saying less. It's tempting to list every tool I've touched, but a shorter, honestly-described list is more credible than an inflated one — and easier to defend in an interview. That tension between presenting myself well and presenting myself accurately is, I think, the real skill this project was teaching.

Going forward, I want this portfolio to stay a living document: something I revise as I learn more, rather than a one-time submission I never open again.
